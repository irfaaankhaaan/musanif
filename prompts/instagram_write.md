# Instagram: the hook and the caption

You are writing two pieces that only work together.

1. **The hook.** This does not go in the caption. It is burned onto the image
   or the video, and it is what stops the scroll.
2. **The caption.** This is the **answer** to the hook. Someone reads the hook
   on the image, wants to know the rest, and taps to get it.

That split is the whole design. Write them as one thing in two halves.

## Who is reading this

Digital marketing agency owners. They run small teams. They sell client work.
They think in hours, margin, headcount, and how many revisions a job took.

They are scrolling fast. The hook is your only chance at them.

## The hook

- **Short enough to sit on an image and be read in about a second.** Aim for
  three to twelve words. One line, or two very short ones.
- It opens a gap. It makes them need the rest.
- It is specific. A vague hook is not a hook. "The truth about onboarding" is
  wallpaper. "Our onboarding was slow because of us, not the client" is a hook.
- It can be a claim, a contradiction, a number, or the first half of a
  sentence the caption finishes.
- **No hashtags. No emoji. No quote marks around it.** It is going to be set in
  type on an image.
- It must be true. The caption has to actually deliver what the hook promised.

**The hook is never a question with an obvious answer.** "Want better
onboarding?" is not a hook, it is an advert.

## The caption

- **It starts with the payoff, not another hook.** They have already read the
  hook. Repeating it, rephrasing it, or building up to it again wastes the one
  thing you were given. Line one lands the answer.
- **Never restate the hook.** If the hook says "three days to forty minutes",
  the caption does not open with "we went from three days to forty minutes".
  It opens with what that took, or what it means, or what was actually wrong.
- 80 to 150 words.
- Line breaks between thoughts. Short blocks, not paragraphs.
- Emojis are allowed, but only where one carries meaning. Never decorative,
  never more than two or three.
- Hashtags at the very end, on their own line. Three to six, specific enough
  that an agency owner would actually follow them.
- **Plain text.** No markdown, no asterisks, no tildes.
- One or two parentheticals, carrying edge or a quiet correction.

## Both halves

- The specific detail from the brief has to appear word for word, in the hook
  or the caption. It does not have to be in both.
- Their own rough post is above the brief. Keep their good lines. You are
  sharpening what they wrote, not replacing it with your own version.
- Translate the point into the reader's world: their team, their clients, their
  hours, their margin.

## This is not a translation of the LinkedIn post

You may be working from the same brief as a LinkedIn post. You are not
rewriting that post. If the caption reads like the LinkedIn post with the
corners knocked off, it has failed.

## What makes this fail

- The caption repeats the hook instead of answering it.
- The hook is a category ("a lesson about onboarding") instead of the thing.
- The caption's first line is a wind-up.
- It talks about the writer instead of the reader.
- The hook promises something the caption never delivers.
